# Workflow scorecard

The handout for Block 4 of the [leaders' session](../README.md). The run of show is in the
[facilitator guide](../FACILITATOR.md#052-block-4-score-your-own-workflows-33-minutes).

Print the scorecard once per person, and once more per table for the scores the table agrees.
Print the example once per table.

---

## The scorecard

**Name:** ______________________  **Table:** ______________________

Take two or three workflows from the slips on your table. First strike out any that a form, a
fixed rule or a search box would solve. Those do not need an agent. Start with the simplest thing
that works.

Score each workflow from 1 to 5 on four things. **5 is always good news for a first pilot.**
Score alone and in silence first. Then compare with your table.

| | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business value** | Saves a few minutes a month. Nobody would notice | Saves a team several hours every week | Moves a number the organisation already reports: cost, waiting time, errors, revenue |
| **The data is ready** | The knowledge is in people's heads, or it is red under the data rule | It is written down, but scattered, out of date or without an owner | Written down, current, one named owner, and green or approved yellow under the data rule |
| **A mistake is cheap** | One wrong output could harm a person, break a law or move money that cannot be recovered | A wrong output reaches a customer or a colleague, and costs an apology and rework | A person sees every output before it matters. A wrong one costs minutes |
| **Easy to undo** | It replaces a system or a team. Going back would take months | Going back takes weeks, and people outside the team would notice | It runs beside today's process. Switch it off in a day and nobody outside notices |

Green, yellow and red are the three levels of the
[data rule](../../docs/FACILITATOR.md#the-data-rule), which is on the board.

### The floors

A high total is not enough. A workflow is **not a first pilot** if:

- **A mistake is cheap** is 1 or 2, or **Easy to undo** is 1 or 2. Park it, and write down why.
- **The data is ready** is 1 or 2. The first project is the data, not the agent.

The highest total that clears the floors is your table's winner. If two are level, take the one
where a mistake is cheaper.

### At your table

1. Compare scores row by row. Where two people are two or more points apart, that row is the
   conversation. The scores are a way to argue, not arithmetic.
2. Agree one score for each row, and write it on the table's copy.
3. Circle the winner. Be ready to say in one minute: its name, its four scores, and the most
   exciting idea your table parked, with the row that sank it.

## An example: three ideas for Ridgeline

Ridgeline Climbing Gym wants an AI on its phone line. Three ideas reach the owner's desk. The
scores below are one reader's judgment, shown to explain the scale. They are not measurements.

| | The agent takes every call, day and night, with no staff on the phone | The agent decides refunds and credits | The agent answers questions from the handbook and books classes after the caller confirms. Everything else goes to staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business value | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| The data is ready | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| A mistake is cheap | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Easy to undo | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| **Total** | 10 | 11 | **18** |
| **Clears the floors?** | No | No | **Yes** |

**Why.** The first idea is the one that sounds best in a meeting. It also puts the agent on the
call about an injured child, with nobody to hand over to, and it removes the staff who would
catch a mistake. The second moves money, and a refund paid in error is hard to take back. The
third is the least exciting. The handbook is written down and has an owner, every booking waits
for the caller, and if it goes badly the calls go back to the front desk the same day.

The third idea is the agent the engineers build in the [one-day workshop](../../README.md). It
has no refund tool and no cancel tool, and its instructions send every injury call to the duty
manager.
