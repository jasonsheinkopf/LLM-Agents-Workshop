# Follow-up emails

Four short messages. They are what turns two good hours into a pilot that starts.

## The next morning: the plan

**Subject:** Yesterday's AI agents session: the plan, and what happens next

Hi all,

Yesterday this group scored [number] workflows, chose one pilot and wrote its plan. A photo of the
plan is attached, with the gate map and the shortlist.

- **The pilot:** [one sentence from row 1 of the plan].
- **The owner:** [name]. **The sponsor:** [name].
- **The review:** [date]. [Owner] brings the answers to the
  [five questions](../../handouts/questions-for-leaders.md), each with something to show, and the
  group decides: go on, change it, or stop.
- **Next in line:** [the shortlist, with the name next to each].

Doubts from the wall that did not become test cases: [list, or "none"]. They are on the agenda
for the review.

## Week one: to the owner

**Subject:** [Pilot name]: the first week

Hi [owner],

Three things turn the plan into a pilot. All three fit in the first week.

1. **Count the baseline.** Row 3 of the plan names the number. If nobody counts it today, count
   it for one week before anything is built. Without it, the review has nothing to compare.
2. **Hand the plan to whoever will build it.** The five test cases, the gate map and the list of
   what the agent will not do are their brief. Ask them to turn the test cases into tests the
   pilot must pass, and to tell you where each gate is enforced: in the system, or only in the
   agent's instructions.
3. **Check the data.** The scorecard gave "the data is ready" a score. Confirm with
   whoever owns that data that it is green, or approved yellow, under the data rule.

If the team that will build it has not built an agent with traces, tests and guardrails before,
the one-day engineers' workshop teaches that pattern, hands-on:
https://github.com/jasonsheinkopf/LLM-Agents-Workshop

[facilitator]

## Day 30: the review

Two messages. The first goes to the owner a week before the review. The second goes to every
leader who was in the room.

**Subject:** [Pilot name]: what to bring to the review on [date]

Hi [owner],

The review is 30 minutes. The group will ask the five questions, so bring something to show for
each:

1. A trace of a wrong answer, and what went wrong.
2. The test set: how many cases, and where they came from.
3. Who grades the answers, and how the grader was checked.
4. What the pilot can do without a person, and what stops it doing more.
5. For the last change that was made: the scores before and after.

And the baseline number, counted again.

Then the group decides: go on, change it, or stop. If the evidence says stop, stopping is a good
result. If the pilot has not started, say where it stalled: that is as useful as a success.

[facilitator]
